Falling Into; Coming to

Pain that Just Won't Let Up

Difference between taking Oxycodone and having to take Tapentadol; Every Difference is A Challenge — Every Inch of Difference predicated on a foundation of Pain and Misery accompanied by crescendo of Silence.

🏗️
When I was on Oxycodone, I felt drowsy but did not want to sleep, solution was rather easy. I just get up and either stretch my legs or make myself a cup of tea. Drowsiness brought on by Oxycodone I can always beat by undertaking any form of mental or physical activity. Drowsiness brought on by tiredness is another matter. For that , the only solution is "sleep".

At home, making a cup of tea is not always an easy choice though since the apartment has no heating so making a cup of tea is the least preferrable option because of the cold although I usually end up making two trips. One to put the kettle on and second to make the cup of tea which usually means that the second trip is only undertaken if I really want that cup of tea as by the end of the first trip to put the kettle on, I am awake.

Since they put me on Tapentadol however, making myself a cup of tea has become academic in terms of options. You see, I don't feel sleepy much less drowsy. I don't have to reluctantly trudge to the kitchen anymore to make tea unless I really want to. I am in pain and I take Tapentadol. I am still in pain though and then I wonder when did I fall asleep because I am opening my eyes. Initially I was really worried because I was missing a couple of stages in between 'pain' and 'eyes opening' such as
  1. early in day
  2. in pain
  3. take tablet
  4. pain diminishes
  5. normal activities resume
  6. evening
  7. pain returns
  8. take pain killer
  9. slow down
  10. dinner
  11. feel drowsy
  12. plug phone
  13. check emails/read book/listen to music
  14. loo
  15. eyes fluttering
  16. relax/eyes close
  17. fall asleep
  18. wake up (open eyes)</p>

Now it may be in the afternoon and I take a oainkiller but I do not feel the benefits of the pain reduction and then my eyes open. All the processes in between have just disappeared and I worry because the events I usually remember i.e. the feeling of drowsiness, the eyes closing and opening; processes I can remember going through since I was a child have literally compacted into one disconcerting one - opening my eyes. To make this even more worrying, over time as I struggled with this phenomeone, I now discover that my eyes are opening more during the daytime and I am not sleeping at night anymore. Then I realise that I am liable to open my eyes anywhere. I lost a good job due to it. It happens in the car so I had to stop driving

Slowly, I realise I am no longer in control of every day events and I can't identify what the cause was until 3 years ago when I was admitted after the carers came in, found me stark naked, confused and rambling. However the Hospital did not bother following up this aberration in my character and simply assumed it was a case of "drugs". Since then, I have narrowed down these issues to the use of Tapentadol but all attempts to get the Pain Doctors to try another opiate has been unsuccessful.

Tapentadol took my Fifi.

You may be wondering - come to? What does it mean? It means I come to after passing out; I don't fall asleep, I pass out and come to; recover and come to.

After passing out in the car and recovering just in time to stop my car careering out of control, I reaslised what was happening and after bringing the car to a halt, I was so frightened of the circumstances and worse, the danger I pposed to thoose around me, I concluded I had to stop driving as soon as I can.

I had always suspected but could not be sure until that fateful day. Falling asleep and passing out are two very different animals. No matter how sudden the onset of sleep is, you lnow the difference. Whilst working at my last job, I kept passing out but to my colleagues, it looked like I was falling asleep, right in the office, in a highly paid job. 

Of course I was fired. I wou;d fire me in those circumstances considering the position, the optics and the environnemnt. Then I was so confused. I was doing a job I loved and in a postion of great responsibility and I was "just sleeping"? All I needed was to sit down and I was gone. It did not matter whether it was morning, noon or evening. As long as I was not physically active, I was gone.

I tried to not "sleep" but no matter, it was just impossible to stop it. I could not understand how I could allow myself to "fall asleep" at work and no matter the effort I put into trying to stop myself. I just could not and when I was fired, I hit rock bottom. If I was drowsy, I could try and stop myself but I just slipped away. First I was cleaning my monitor; then my eyes were opening. I hated myself.

When I Sherlock Holmed myself, the only medication I had not used before this strange malady was Tapentadol and when in August 2023 I managed to confirm that it was indeed Tapentadol, imagine my anger and frustration when one of the consultants I tried to explain my problems to - a guy with a name that sounded like "The Crook" responded curtly that "I was trying to pressurise him" to change my medication. 

Was I surprised? No! I was just irritatated. I had expeerienced this Doctors lack of empathy first hand during my admission. After I was transferred to Genmark Hill from the PRUH, I had a relapse and his tratment option? "Dry me out" and compel other consultants to make sure I did not receive any pain medication.

I have spent a large part of my life in and around Doctors and Hospitals and I can say with all certainty that KCH is easily the worst I have attended so far. I found better care and carng at Queen Elizabeth II in Woolwich. From the staff to the Pharmacists to the Consultants - everybody was professional , caring and showed great emparhy. Not so at KCH.

There is a colossal lack of empathy. Whilst all at Woolwich displayed great empathy and resourcefulness, it was the exception at KCH. There, most showed a lack of it. That says a lot about the attitude. 

Even the non medical staff at KCH showed how pervasive this lack of empathy was. I asked the FOI officer at Kings College Hospital a copy of their drugs policy. This was November 2025 and they turned it into a game. If a patient is not qualified to receive and read the Drugs policy, then they are just confirming my statement that they are mostly elitist and arrogant. 

Of course you can't paint the whole staff with one brush but so far after a decade, I will rate Woolwich higher than Denmark Hill with one exception - the senior administartive management team failed Woolwich. The picture at KCH is murkier. It seems the rot is infecting all levels but I have to say - the MRI team at KCH - Only one team has failed so far. The rest are rated excellent.
